## Misses

* PR-E2-1: predicted "P3 forced shutdowns at most half of P0's"; observed P3 0.4, P0 0.767.
  Miss. Madkhal reduced forced shutdowns relative to always going, but by less than half. Most blooms in G1 are invisible to the broadband detector (the visible share follows the hit rate posterior, roughly one in three blooms), and a decision cannot react to a bloom it cannot see. This is the central limitation of a satellite only advisory, not a tuning issue.
* PR-E2-4: predicted "P3 between 5 and 40 DERATE days per year"; observed 3.77.
  Miss. Madkhal derated on fewer days per year than predicted. Because most blooms raise no alert, the belief rarely leaves the seasonal prior, and the prediction overestimated how often it would cross the feasibility threshold.
* PR-E3-1: predicted "Every policy except the oracle has higher regret on G2 than on G1"; observed higher on G2: P0, P2, P3, P4.
  Miss. The alert_park rule did better on G2 than on G1, not worse. Its cost is dominated by unserved water while parked after alerts, and G2's longer blind season leaves fewer alerts to react to, so it parks less. The prediction assumed every policy would suffer from the stress world; a rule that mostly harms itself on alerts gains when alerts are rarer.

## Hits that need qualifying

* PR-E1-3: A hit, but a weak one: coverage equal to one means the intervals are wider than they need to be on G1. That is expected, because G1's truth sits at the posterior mean, the centre of the draws. Coverage on a world whose parameters are far from the posterior mean is not measured here.
* PR-E1-4: A hit on very little evidence: only the two lowest forecast bins held enough days to qualify under the preregistered rule. The sparse upper bins suggest overconfidence at the top end (the highest bin's observed frequency is below its mean forecast), which this prediction did not test.
* PR-E2-3: A hit on the means only. The paired regret intervals of Madkhal against always going and against the seasonal rule both include zero on G1, so the ordering among those three policies is not established; only the large gap to alert_park is.
* PR-E3-3: A hit that counts against Madkhal: on the stress world the fixed seasonal rule had lower mean regret than Madkhal without inspection. The paired interval of that difference includes zero, so the ordering is not established either way.
* PR-E4-1: A hit on the mean only: the interval of the difference includes zero, so the evidence that surveys pay at this cost is weak.
* PR-E7-2: Not blind: the Kalba replay window around the one detected event was seen during development before the preregistration (disclosed there).

## What these results do not show

* The plant is hypothetical; no result here says anything about a real plant.
* G1 shares the estimator's assumptions, so E1 and E2 are optimistic by construction; G2 is
  the stress test, and it is still written by the same author.
* The hit rate rests on one detected event, and event dates are mostly upper bounds.
* Costs are hypothetical units; the weight sensitivity shows how rankings move with them.
* Nothing here is validated against a real plant or real operator logs.
* Intervals are mean plus or minus 1.96 standard errors, as preregistered; for skewed,
  mostly zero counts they can extend below zero, which no count can reach.
